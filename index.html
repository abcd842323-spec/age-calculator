How your age is calculated

Your age is the exact time between your date of birth and the “as of” date, counted the way official forms count it: full years first, then full months, then the remaining days. Months have different lengths (28–31 days) and leap years add a 29th day to February, so this calculator borrows days from the real calendar instead of assuming every month has 30 days — the answer matches what a government or exam form expects.

Worked example: born 15 August 2000, age as of 25 November 2026 = 26 years, 3 months, 10 days, and the next birthday falls on Sunday, 15 August 2027, turning 27.

Born on 29 February? In a year without a 29 February, your birthday is counted on 1 March for the next-birthday countdown.

Common questions

Why does my age differ by a day on some sites?
Some calculators count the birth day itself, or treat every month as 30 days. This one uses the standard calendar method used on official forms, so results can differ by a day from rough calculators.
How do I find my age on a specific cut-off date?
Change the “Age as of” date to the cut-off date printed on the form (for example 1 January of the exam year) and calculate again.
Does this work for a date in the past?
Yes — set “Age as of” to any past date after the birth date to see the age on that day.
